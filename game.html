Abstract

This interactive narrative explores how late-night screen exposure affects circadian rhythms, sleep architecture, and morning cognitive performance. Set at 11:00 PM, players step into the shoes of a tired student faced with a bedtime decision: engage with short-form video notifications on a smartphone or put the screen away to read a physical book under warm lighting. Through branching path choices, the story contrasts the physiological consequences of blue light exposure against optimal sleep hygiene practices. Choosing the smartphone path leads the player through a "scrolling loop," where short-wavelength blue light suppresses melatonin production and delays sleep readiness, leading to lost deep sleep cycles, morning grogginess, and reduced alertness. Conversely, choosing the book corner facilitates natural melatonin onset, resulting in restful sleep and a refreshed, full cognitive state the following morning. By transforming sleep research into an interactive experience, this narrative highlights the tangible, everyday choices that govern our sleep health and mental wellness. here but that's okay.
